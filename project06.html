Project 6: Simple Car

For this project, I built a simple robot car using as few pieces as possible while still making it sturdy and reliable. The assignment asked us to think like both an economist and an engineer: what could be removed to simplify the design, and what had to stay to keep the car structurally sound? I focused on a lightweight frame, efficient wheel placement, and a design that would still hold up under repeated testing.

The goal was not just to make the car move, but to make it move in controlled ways. The final car was able to drive forward and backward at different speeds, spin in both directions, and follow both small and large arcs. Those motions required balancing speed, stability, and traction, because a design that is too loose can wobble, while a design that is too heavy or overbuilt can slow everything down.

By testing different layouts and small adjustments, I improved the car’s movement and made its behavior more predictable. This project showed how a simple mechanism can still be a meaningful engineering problem: the challenge is not only getting a machine to move, but designing it to move well with the least unnecessary material and the strongest possible structure.
